Model names look like decoration. They are a lab’s self-portrait, and by the end you will know why one company calls its biggest model Mythos and another calls it Carbon.

You open a model picker and find Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, Fable. Or Astra, Sol, Terra, Luna. Or Carbon, Hydrogen, Argon, Neon. The list reads like internal codenames that escaped into production. None of the names says what the model does. Every one of them says something about the company that chose it.

Anthropic names models after things you write

Start with the names you already know. Haiku, Sonnet, Opus: three forms of writing, in increasing order of ambition. A haiku fits in seventeen syllables. A sonnet takes fourteen lines. An opus is a whole composed work, the sort you buy a ticket to hear. Anthropic added two more tiers in 2026, and both are stories: Fable and Mythos. Mythos is the one kept behind a verification program; Fable is the public version with the guardrails left on.

Read the ladder in order and it becomes a theory of scale told in literary forms. Short poem, long poem, whole work, story, creation story. The company that sells you a model also sells you the feeling that you are climbing toward something written. At this rate Claude 7 will be an epic.

OpenAI names models after things in the sky

Anthropic reaches for the shelf marked human expression. OpenAI walks outside and points up. Astra, Sol, Luna, Terra: the stars, the sun, the moon, and the rock they are all sitting on. The number does the boring work — GPT-6.2, GPT-6.1, GPT-6 — and the name does the fun work. Numbers make a model sound like a build. Astra makes it sound like a destination.

Terra is the odd one out, the only name in the set under your feet instead of above your head. It is also the model OpenAI describes for visual reasoning and precise computer use. The one named for the ground is the one that watches your screen.

Google names models after what everything is made of

Google went a level deeper than the sky, to what the sky and everything in it is made of. The Gemini 4 line is Carbon, Neon, Hydrogen, Argon: four elements from the periodic table. Carbon is the flagship. Hydrogen is the one for everyone. Argon is the efficient one. Neon arrived last.

The table does the job a size label would do, but it lets Google call its flagship carbon, which sounds like a material rather than a price tier. Then there is Nano Banana, the image model, named after a nickname for a product manager. Google’s lineup has two columns: things made of atoms, and a banana. You can tell which ones it takes seriously.

Meta and xAI name models after things you can feel

Meta used to call its family Llama, after an animal. Its frontier line is now Muse — Wonder, Spark, Ember, Glimmer — named for the Greek goddesses of the arts and then given names that sound like things you would write a poem about. Meta started with a llama and arrived in Anthropic’s poetry section by another route.

xAI took the opposite path. One name, Grok: a verb coined by Heinlein for understanding something so completely that it becomes part of you. Then a version number, 4.7. No family to memorise, no tier to decode. Musk named a feeling and shipped version 4.7 of it.

Chinese labs name models after what they do

Chinese labs take a third path. Their model names read like part numbers. Alibaba’s family is Qwen, short for Tongyi Qianwen — a name that means something like “comprehend the meaning, answer a thousand questions” — followed by a version and a job: Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, Qwen4-Max, Qwen3-Coder. DeepSeek takes its company name from “deep seeking” and calls its models R1, V3.1, V4-Pro, V4.1-Flash. Z.ai ships GLM-4.7-FlashX. Moonshot ships Kimi K3.1 Pro Highspeed. ByteDance’s Doubao family has models called Seedance and Seedream.

Where a US lab picks a mood, a Chinese lab picks a spec sheet. The number gives you the generation; the suffix gives you the size or the task. Pro, Flash, Max, Coder, Omni: words from a product catalogue. None of them asks you to picture the moon. The model still does the same work. Only the pitch changes.

Poems. Planets. Elements. Muses. Verbs. Specs. None. Measure. Anything. Benchmarks. Measure. Something.
