When UMAP Lies, Part 3: Settings, Seeds and Starting Points

Settings move a method about as far as switching methods, the start decides the large distances, and some of what changes between seeds is real

Every method in the series tuned on a grid of 4 to 12 settings, chosen on one draw of the data and scored on others; what UMAP’s n_neighbors and min_dist change and what they only rearrange; initialisation, which decides large distances and leaves neighbourhoods alone; and five seeds on three draws, separating rotation from real change and the method’s randomness from the data’s.

The settings, the starting layout and the random seed of a UMAP or t-SNE run all change the map it draws, and some of those changes are as large as switching to a different method.

Anyone who has re-run UMAP has seen a different picture: the islands in new places, one of them now upside down, a bridge that was there before now gone. Some of that is meaningless, since a map rotated or mirrored is the same map. Some of it is not. A comparison of methods that ignores this can rank the methods by their defaults instead of by what they can do, and a reader who ignores it can take one run’s arrangement for a property of the data.

This is the third of four parts. Part 1 has the metrics and methods; Part 2 ambient and intrinsic dimension; Part 4 gaps, density and holes.

This part covers three ideas:

  • A fair comparison tunes every method on a comparable grid and scores it on data it was not tuned on. Tuned that way, most of the defaults’ differences in global structure disappear.
  • The starting layout decides the large distances and leaves the neighbourhoods alone.
  • Seed-to-seed change has two parts: a rotation or reflection that means nothing, and a rearrangement that alignment cannot remove. Only the second matters, and it differs by method.

1 Design

1.1 Data

Three synthetic datasets of 2,000 points, with three independent draws each:

  • Swiss roll: the flat sheet rolled in 3 dimensions of Part 2, no noise. Truth: the flat sheet.
  • Clusters: ten Gaussian clusters in 10 dimensions, nearest centres 6 standard deviations apart, sizes unequal (the largest four times the smallest). Truth: the clusters and their labels.
  • Tree: a trunk with three branches that each split in two, straight segments in 10 dimensions with noise of standard deviation 0.03. Truth: distance along the tree.

1.2 Grids

Each method gets a grid around its defaults, from 4 settings for the methods with one main parameter to 12 for t-SNE, counting the default once. The grids vary the parameters each method’s documentation names as its main ones; methods with more parameters get larger grids, which favours them slightly in the selection.

Code 
from umaplab.embeddings import METHODS as _M


def R_is_default(m, g):
    """True when a grid entry equals the method's defaults on every key it sets."""
    d = _M[m].defaults
    return all(k in d and d[k] == v for k, v in g.items())


rows = []
for m, grid in X.TUNE_GRIDS.items():
    keys = sorted({k for g in grid for k in g})
    vals = "; ".join(f"{k} ∈ {{{', '.join(str(v) for v in sorted({g[k] for g in grid if k in g}, key=str))}}}" for k in keys)
    distinct = {json.dumps(g, sort_keys=True) for g in grid}
    n_set = len(distinct) + (0 if any(R_is_default(m, g) for g in grid) else 1)
    rows.append((P.name(m), n_set, vals))
md(pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["Method", "Settings", "Values"]))

1.3 Selection

Choosing the best setting and reporting its score on the same data flatters every method with a grid, and flatters most the method with the largest grid. Here each method’s best setting for each metric is chosen on draw 0 and scored on draws 1 and 2, which it has not seen. Two metrics are used: 10-neighbour preservation against the truth (local) and the Spearman correlation of map distances with true distances (global; on the Swiss roll and the tree, the true distances run along the surface).

def span(df, gen, mth):
    cm = df[(df.generator == gen) & (df.method == mth)].groupby("config").value.median()

  • On global structure, a method’s own settings can move it as far as switching methods. On the Swiss roll, the distance correlation of t-SNE spans 0.68 across its grid, TriMap’s 0.64 and Laplacian eigenmaps’ 0.61; the spread across all eight methods at their defaults is 0.72. On the tree, UMAP’s own range is 0.58 against a between-method spread of 0.60.
  • Local structure moves less, except where a setting changes the neighbourhood size the method works at. t-SNE’s local score on the Swiss roll spans 0.60 and Isomap’s 0.59; UMAP’s spans 0.12.

2.2 Defaults against tuned

  • Tuned, most methods keep the Swiss roll’s large distances about equally well. At defaults, the eight methods with settings span 0.27–1.00 in distance correlation; tuned, they span 0.89–1.00. TriMap goes from 0.54 to 0.96 with 40 inlier neighbours, and UMAP from 0.93 to 0.98 with 200 neighbours out of 2,000 points (on 1,000 points 200 is too many; see UMAP’s two settings). A default-settings comparison would have reported TriMap and Laplacian eigenmaps as poor at global structure; the defaults were poor, not the methods.
  • On the clusters, PCA and TriMap keep the arrangement best, tuned or not. Their correlations are 0.87 and 0.88; tuned t-SNE reaches 0.76 (at perplexity 300) and tuned UMAP 0.67.
  • Tuning can lose. For global structure, in 2 of the 24 method–dataset pairs with a grid, the setting chosen on draw 0 did worse on draws 1–2 than the default, by up to 0.11; for local structure, in 0. Choosing among settings on the data being reported can overstate how good the chosen plot is, though across these grids the overstatement was small.

2.3 UMAP’s two settings

UMAP’s n_neighbors sets how many neighbours define each point’s neighbourhood; min_dist sets how close points may sit on the map. A finer grid of the two (seven values by six, three seeds each, 1,000 points) shows what each one changes.

  • On the clusters, both settings lower local structure, and neither steers the arrangement. Ten-neighbour preservation falls from 0.40 at 5 neighbours to 0.27 at 200 (min_dist 0), and from 0.40 to 0.32 as min_dist goes from 0 to 0.99 (5 neighbours). The distance correlation, which measures how the clusters are arranged, varies between 0.35 and 0.57 over the grid with no trend in either setting. Three seeds at one setting differ by a median of 0.16 in it, and by 0.008 in local preservation: where the clusters sit relative to each other is decided by the run, not the setting.
  • On the Swiss roll, n_neighbors decides whether the sheet holds together. At 5 neighbours UMAP tears the sheet into a dozen short fragments; from about 30 it draws one continuous strip. The distance correlation rises from 0.82 at 5 neighbours to 0.99 at 50, and falls again at 200 (0.76), most of all with a large min_dist (0.57 at 0.99). At 200 the strip curls into a spiral. A neighbourhood of 200 out of 1,000 points is a fifth of the sheet, wide enough to reach across the roll’s layers in the 3-D data, which is one likely reason; this part does not test it.
  • min_dist is not only cosmetic. On the Swiss roll, raising it from 0.1 to 0.5 at 15 neighbours raises local preservation from 0.79 to 0.86, because points packed less tightly on the map keep their order within the sheet; on the clusters it lowers it.

The widget shows the maps behind the grid. On the clusters, change the seed at any setting: the islands trade places and the global tile moves, by as much as 0.4, while the local tile moves by two hundredths at most. On the Swiss roll, drag n_neighbors from 5 to 50 and the torn fragments join into one flat strip; go on to 200 with min_dist at 0.99 and the strip curls into a spiral, its ends wrapping round each other.

3 Starting points

Kobak & Linderman (2021) found that UMAP’s apparent advantage over t-SNE in global structure came from its spectral start, and that random starts damage both. The runs here agree on both points, with one exception, and add one.

  • The start does not change local structure. UMAP’s 10-neighbour preservation on the Swiss roll is 0.77 from a spectral start and 0.77 from a random one; t-SNE’s is 0.85 either way.
  • A random start lowers global structure and makes it erratic, except for t-SNE on the Swiss roll. On the clusters, UMAP’s distance correlation falls from a median of 0.56 to 0.27 and t-SNE’s from 0.65 to 0.33. On the Swiss roll, UMAP from random starts ranges from 0.23 to 0.98 over 15 runs. t-SNE on the Swiss roll is the exception: from random starts it keeps the large distances as well as from its PCA start (median 0.97 against 0.95).
  • An informative start carries its own view of the shape. PCA of a Swiss roll is the rolled-up spiral, so UMAP started from PCA keeps less of the unrolled sheet’s distances than UMAP started from its spectral layout, which follows the graph along the sheet. “Use an informative start” is right; which start is informative depends on the data.

4 Seeds

4.1 Design

Three draws of the Swiss roll and of the unequal clusters, each embedded with five seeds by UMAP, t-SNE, PaCMAP, TriMap and PHATE, and by UMAP and t-SNE from random starts. The design separates two sources of variation: a different draw of the data (what a replicate experiment would give) and a different seed on the same data (what re-running gives). Repeated runs on one draw are not independent samples of the data-generating process, so they are never pooled with the draws.

Code 
rows = []
for gen in ["swiss_roll", "gaussian_clusters"]:
    for cfg in ["umap", "tsne", "pacmap", "trimap", "phate", "umap·init-random", "tsne·init-random"]:
        for m, k, lab in [("knn_preservation", 10.0, "Local"), ("dist_spearman", None, "Global")]:
            v = R.pick(ST[(ST.generator == gen) & (ST.config == cfg)], m, k, "truth")
            if v.empty:
                continue
            within = np.sqrt(v.groupby("data_seed").value.var(ddof=1).mean())
            between_ = v.groupby("data_seed").value.mean().std(ddof=1)
            rows.append([GEN[gen], P.name(cfg.split("·")[0]) + (", random start" if "random" in cfg else ""), lab, f"{between_:.3f}", f"{within:.3f}"])
VAR = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["Data", "Method", "Score", "Between draws", "Between seeds"])
md(VAR)

4.2 Rotation and real change

Two maps that differ by a rotation, a reflection or a change of scale show the same thing. Each pair of seeds on the same draw is therefore compared after aligning one map onto the other (Procrustes), and with two measures that alignment cannot affect: the share of each point’s 10 map neighbours the two maps share, and the rank correlation of their distances.

  • t-SNE from its PCA start keeps its arrangement between seeds. After alignment its disparity is 0.001 on the Swiss roll and 0.029 on the clusters. Inside each cluster it still changes: two seeds share 0.58 of each point’s 10 map neighbours.
  • UMAP rearranges the clusters from seed to seed, even from its spectral start. Its disparity on the clusters is 0.66 and the rank correlation of distances between two seeds’ maps is 0.49: which cluster sits next to which changes when the seed does. On the connected Swiss roll it changes much less (0.083).
  • PaCMAP and TriMap fix the arrangement but not the inside of each cluster. Their maps from two seeds agree almost perfectly on large distances (0.99 and 0.99), but share only 0.17 and 0.17 of each point’s 10 map neighbours. A 10-dimensional cluster has no preferred 2-D layout, and each run picks one.
  • Random starts change the arrangement of both UMAP and t-SNE (disparity 0.83 for UMAP and 0.79 for t-SNE on the clusters).

A reader comparing two runs should discount orientation entirely. The relative positions of clusters are a property of the run for UMAP at defaults and for any method from a random start, and a property of the data only where several seeds agree on them.

5 Constraints

  • Three datasets, three draws. The tuning results are for a rolled sheet, separated Gaussian clusters and a noisy tree, at \(n = 2000\) (1,000 in the UMAP grid). Tuned rankings on real data depend on its structure.
  • Grids are coarse and unequal. From 4 to 12 settings per method can miss a better region, and the larger grids give their methods more chances in the selection; the comparison is not exhaustive.
  • Selection on one draw is noisy. Choosing on draw 0 and scoring on draws 1–2 removes the optimism of reporting the chosen score, but one draw is a small selection set, which is part of why some tuned settings did worse.
  • Two metrics drive the selection. A setting chosen for global structure can lose local structure, and the reverse; the table shows each metric’s choice separately, and no setting was chosen for both.
  • UMAP with a PCA start ran with one seed per draw; the other starting points ran with five.
  • Learning rate and early exaggeration were varied for t-SNE only at single values (\(\text{early exaggeration} \in \{4, 36\}\); learning rate at openTSNE’s automatic setting), so this part says little about them.

Tune. Every. Method. Starts. Set. Distances. Seeds. Move. Clusters.
